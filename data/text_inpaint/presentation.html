Erasing the sonographer's notes

A Noise2Noise U-Net trained to remove burned-in annotation — NIPPLE, CLIP, SCAR, clock positions — from inside the ultrasound region, while leaving the scanner's own chrome untouched. Every figure below comes from 227 frames the model never trained on, carrying real annotation rather than the pasted stamps it learned from.

How much of the word survives

Contrast is the word's box mean minus the mean of the ring around it, in grey levels on the 0–255 scale the frames are stored in. A word that started 28 levels above its surroundings and ends 9 above it has lost roughly two thirds of what made it legible.

The distribution, not the average

Remaining contrast is |after| ÷ |before|. Zero means the word is gone; one means the model left it alone. The mass sits low, but the tail at one is real and is what the worst cases below show.

How it was trained

Text has no twin frames the way calipers do — there is no clean re-acquisition of the same view with the word missing. Noise2Noise sidesteps that. Take a frame verified free of annotation, paste a random set of harvested glyph stamps to make the input, paste a different independently drawn set to make the target, and train with L1. At a pixel the input stamp covers, the target is bare tissue most of the time, so the optimal output is the tissue underneath.

Anything identical on both sides is preserved, because the loss punishes changing it. That is the whole reason the scanner sidebar survives: stamps land only inside the ultrasound rectangle, and crops are taken from the whole frame, so the network sees chrome-with-no-edit and tissue-with-edit side by side.

The stamps carry an alpha channel built from a morphological top-hat, so a paste composites the glyph and not a rectangle of donor tissue. Roughly 60% of each stamp's bounding box is fully transparent.

What this does not tell you

  • Nothing here was measured against the classifier. Contrast is measured on the full-resolution frame. Whether the residue is still legible to the model at 256 px is a separate question, and answering it means re-running the occlusion test on detexted crops.
  • Seven percent of removal targets are untouched. Not faint — unchanged. On a corpus rebuild those frames would carry their annotation through.
  • RIB and IMPLANT are thin. Eleven and eight stamps against NIPPLE's 328, because OCR found so few. They are represented, not learned.
  • The training pool is the OCR'd sample, 13,506 of roughly 558,000 v7 frames, because those are the only frames with a verdict on whether they were already clean.
